# O1: dark-region blocking is the video encoder's (2026-09-27)

The owner's O1 (`2026-09-26_distill_run_predictions.md`): the distills render
dark regions "blocky and splotchy". `bench/o1_lossless_blocking.py` decodes
the first 7 latent frames (22 video frames) of `look_anchor__pdd8`'s saved
latent straight to pixels on the CPU, through `h3_config.MODELS["video_vae"]`,
and compares the block-edge statistic with the same frames of its mp4.
Record: `2026-09-26_o1_lossless_anchor_pdd8.json`. One clip, one scene.

| | dark 8 px | bright 8 px | dark 16 px | bright 16 px |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lossless (VAE output) | 1.018 | 0.957 | 0.961 | 0.963 |
| mp4 | 1.156 | 0.962 | 1.085 | 0.938 |

- **The model and VAE output show no dark blocking excess.** The dark
  ratios are at or below the bright ones.
- **The mp4 adds it**, in dark regions only: +0.14 at 8 px and +0.12 at 16 px.
  The mean luma difference, mp4 against lossless, is 1.1 levels.
- **The encoder.** Every generated graph saves through `VHS_VideoCombine` as
  `video/h264-mp4`, `yuv420p`, `crf` 19: 8-bit 4:2:0 at VHS's default
  quality, which is where h264 bands and blocks dark gradients.

So what: O1's splotchy blacks are a save setting, not a distill defect.
Candidates: a lower `crf` (quality at the cost of size), or a 10-bit format
(`video/h265-mp4` with a 10-bit `pix_fmt`, if the owner's players take it).
This is a house setting in `workflows/h3_config.py` and the generator, so
it is the owner's call. The test's caveat: 22 frames of one PDD8 clip. A
second scene, and a FlashGen clip (the distill that lifts blacks), would
confirm it.
