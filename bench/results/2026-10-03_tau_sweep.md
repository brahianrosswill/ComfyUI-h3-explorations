# The live tau sweep: instrument and premises (2026-10-03)

**Status: the instrument is built and its premises are measured. No
calibration has run and no table ships.** The owner put the output check
ahead of the calibration on 2026-10-03 (`2026-10-03_sol_output_check.md`): a
table calibrated on local error has nothing that could accept it yet. The
first full-size sweep render was stopped a quarter of the way in when that
decision arrived; a partial record is not a calibration and was not used.

**Layout.** Everything here ran on the shared-view layout: the reference
still goes to the video VAE and to the text encoder at the same 2048 short
edge, so one still contributes its latent rows and about as many encoder
tokens again. The owner made a 512 encoder view the default for reference
stills later the same day; on that layout the conditioning prefix of a
one-reference graph is about half as long, and a timing, a routed share or a
table taken here describes a different shape.

## What was built

- `sol_tau_sweep.py`, armed by `H3_SOL_SWEEP`. On every attention call under
  the sparse node it runs the chained fallback once and Sol at each tau of a
  grid on the same q, k and v, records per segment and head the fallback's
  energy, the squared error at each tau and the routed key blocks at each
  tau, and hands the model the fallback's output. One render therefore
  measures every block and step of both stages at every tau on identical
  inputs. Its docstring owns the record's fields.
- `bench/run_tau_sweep.py` queues the scenes of a sweep: a donor graph's
  prompt and reference chain on one base graph, built in memory.
- `bench/calibrate_sparse_table.py` turns sweep records into a table per
  stage: equal error to the shipped tau per block with fewest routed blocks,
  or equal routed blocks with lowest error, under a per-head guard, with a
  held-out margin. Its docstring owns the method. Exercised on the smoke
  record only.

## Premises

`bench/measure_tau_sweep_premises.py` on three cells of the 2026-09-27 ref2va
capture (two references, 120,582 tokens), `2026-10-03_tau_sweep_premises.json`.
The swept call routes every query row; the shipped call keeps the
conditioning rows exact.

**A query block's output does not depend on which other rows run exact.**
Video rows from the first whole video block on are bit-identical between the
two calls on all three cells. So the sweep's video rows are the shipped
call's, and its conditioning rows give each segment's routed error in the
same pass. The query block that straddles the end of the conditioning prefix
is the one difference: its video rows are exact in a shipped call.

**The fallback is a sufficient reference.** Pooled over video rows, relative
L2:

| cell | dense INT8 against stock bf16 | Sol at tau 1.0 against dense INT8 | against stock |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| block 0, step 2 | 0.0042 | 0.0722 | 0.0721 |
| block 24, step 2 | 0.0076 | 0.1144 | 0.1148 |
| block 49, step 2 | 0.0144 | 0.0670 | 0.0669 |

Sol's error reads the same against either reference at every tau of the
grid, to the third decimal. The shipped tau sits 5 to 17 times above the
dense kernel's own error, which is why "no more error than the dense kernel
carries" is not a usable budget and the calibrator's budget is the shipped
tau's own error.

**Kernel time of one call** (synchronised, this card, same cells): dense INT8
about 0.91 s, stock bf16 attention 2.57 s, Sol with every row routed from
0.19 s at tau 3.0 to 0.72 s at tau 0.25 (block 0). The shipped call, rows
exact, tau 1.0: 0.48 to 0.58 s.

**Error is not smooth in tau everywhere.** Block 49 goes from 0.084 at tau
1.25 to 0.172 at 1.5.

## Through the node

One capture cell through `make_override` with the sweep armed (scratch
harness, session folder): both the tensor entry and core's container entry
return the fallback's bytes; the record's tau 1.0 row equals a direct kernel
call (relative difference under 1e-8 in the sums); routed counts equal the
kernel's `blk_cnt`; a block listed in `dense_blocks` is still swept. Routed
error by segment at tau 1.0 on block 24: text 0.175, reference 0.084, audio
0.175, video 0.114. One cell.

A 49-frame smoke render through an armed server wrote one cell for every
block and step of both samplers. It proves the harness, nothing else: 49
frames is not a canvas anyone renders.

## What this does not show

- No full-size sweep exists. Nothing here says what a table would save.
- The sweep's error is local, per call, against the dense kernel, on the
  fallback's trajectory. Local error did not predict the owner's verdicts on
  2026-10-02.
- The premises are three cells of one capture at one step.
